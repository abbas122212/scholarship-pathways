# SOPHAS MPH Shortlist — the Sakib model, verified (3 Nov 2026)

For: Dr Abbas Uddin Chowdhury, MBBS intern, Bangladesh — Fall 2027 MPH via SOPHAS.
Method: official pages only (SOPHAS/Liaison help centre, ASPPH, DHS Study in the States, university pages). Aggregator figures were used as leads only and are never cited as evidence. Anything not on an official page is marked NOT FOUND.

## Bottom line

- **Yes, SOPHAS is worth using despite modest research experience.** MPH is a professional degree; none of the 12 programmes below lists research experience as an admission requirement. Research experience matters mainly for (a) the most competitive merit money and (b) landing RA jobs after arrival — it is not the gate for admission itself.
- **The portal is one pipe, not one application form for everything.** One SOPHAS account, one transcript/WES set, reused across all designated programmes — but several schools add a supplemental application and fee on top (USF, UIC, UW-type arrangements), and each programme has its own deadline inside the SOPHAS cycle.
- **Jobs while studying are real but fenced.** In year 1, an F-1 student may only work **on campus** (which includes university RA/TA assistantships) at **≤20 hrs/week in term**. Off-campus jobs (e.g. a hospital or VA post not part of the university) need CPT after **one full academic year**, or OPT after graduation. Sakib's Emory University job is an on-campus-type employer if he was enrolled at Emory; a standalone VA-type job is a CPT/OPT matter — do not plan year-1 income around off-campus work.
- **Filter programmes by money mechanics, not prestige:** (1) assistantships/scholarships explicitly open to international students, (2) a priority deadline that is also the *funding* deadline, (3) whether a tuition waiver attaches to assistantships (UIC, Georgia State), (4) cost of the city. Every one of the 12 deadlines below is still open for Fall 2027 as of 3 Nov 2026 — none has passed.

## 1. SOPHAS basics (official)

- **Scale:** ASPPH (which runs SOPHAS) states: **120+ academic institutions** and **3,500+ public health programmes** participate; "With one centralized application, you can explore and apply to multiple participating programs, whether you are a U.S. or international applicant." Source: https://aspph.org/student-journey/sophas/
- **Fees:** **$155 for the first programme, $65 for each additional programme.** Some programmes charge an extra supplemental fee. Source (SOPHAS Applicant Help Center, updated 2026-08-06): https://help.liaisonedu.com/SOPHAS_Applicant_Help_Center/Starting_Your_SOPHAS_Application/Getting_Started_with_Your_SOPHAS_Application/03_Application_Fees
- **Cycle dates (2026–2027 cycle = Fall 2027 entry):** opened **12 August 2026**; the cycle closes **2 August 2027, 11:59 PM ET** — but individual programme deadlines govern and are much earlier. Source: https://help.liaisonedu.com/SOPHAS_Applicant_Help_Center/Starting_Your_SOPHAS_Application/Getting_Started_with_Your_SOPHAS_Application/02_Application_Cycle_Dates
- **One transcript set:** transcripts and the WES evaluation are sent to SOPHAS once and distributed to all designated programmes. Caveats found on official pages: USF also requires the separate USF graduate application + fee; UIC requires a supplemental application + $70 fee; Tulane asks for the credential evaluation to be sent to its own admissions office as well (see school notes).

## 2. SOPHAS fee waiver for international applicants — YES

- SOPHAS runs a Fee Assistance Program: "a limited number of fee waivers are provided to qualified applicants on a first-come, first-served basis. Each fee waiver covers only the initial application fee" ($155; the $65 per extra programme is still payable).
- **International-based waiver:** "To qualify for an international-based fee waiver, you must be a citizen of **and** currently reside in one of the countries on the United Nations Least Developed Countries List." **Bangladesh is on the list printed on SOPHAS's own page.** The same form can request an **$80 coupon off the WES evaluation**.
- Rules that bite: request the waiver **before** submitting (requests after submission are not honoured); if approved, you must submit within **14 days** or the waiver is void; one waiver type per cycle.
- Status check: Bangladesh's LDC graduation was deferred by the UN General Assembly (30 Oct 2026) to November 2029, so LDC-based eligibility is not about to lapse mid-cycle. Source for the waiver: SOPHAS help centre fees page above.

## 3. F-1 employment rules (official US government wording)

- **On-campus, in term:** "If you participate in on-campus employment, you may not work more than 20 hours per week when school is in session." You may apply for on-campus employment up to 30 days before classes start. (DHS, Study in the States, F-1 Postsecondary guide: https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/guide/f-1/f-1-postsecondary)
- **On-campus, breaks:** full-time work is permitted during scheduled breaks/annual vacation (8 CFR 214.2(f)(9)(i) position as stated on university ISSS pages, e.g. Georgia State ISSS: "On-campus employment is limited to 20 hours per week (including assistantships) during the school year. Full-time employment is permitted during summer and vacation periods." — https://isss.gsu.edu/current-students/f-1-students/f-1-employment/)
- **RA/TA = on-campus employment:** yes. University of Florida International Center (official): "Student assistantships/fellowships are considered *on-campus employment* and are therefore subject to the same cumulative 20-hour weekly limit." Georgia State ISSS says the same ("including assistantships"). So an RA/TA at your own university is legal from the first semester, inside the 20-hour cap.
- **CPT:** "A DSO can authorize CPT for an F-1 student … if the student: Has been lawfully enrolled on a full-time basis for at least a full academic year." CPT must be an integral part of the curriculum (e.g. a required practicum) and is authorised per employer. Warning on the same DHS page: "One year of full-time CPT eliminates a student's eligibility for OPT." (https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/sevis-help-hub/student-records/fm-student-employment/f-1-curricular-practical-training-cpt)
- **OPT:** 12 months of work authorisation per education level, in a job directly related to the major, authorised by USCIS with an EAD; can be used after graduation (post-completion OPT is the standard route into a first US research job). (DHS, same CPT page comparison table and Study in the States training pages.)

## 5. Reading the table — where the realistic chances are

- **Admission realism for an MBBS intern with manuscripts under review:** MPH cohorts routinely include physicians; the degree itself is the credential these programmes screen for, plus GPA conversion (WES), English scores and the statement. The public universities (Georgia State, UIC, USF, UAB, Albany) and Drexel/Tulane are the realistic-admission band; Emory, Columbia, BU, GW and Brown are competitive for everyone, and their *top* merit tiers are the real long shots — admission there is plausible, Sakib-scale money is the stretch.
- **Where the Sakib mechanics actually exist:** tuition-waiver assistantships (UIC, Georgia State), a paid-work programme bolted onto the degree (Emory REAL, up to $3,000/semester), an RA-ship packaged with a tuition scholarship (Drexel Research Fellowship), and automatic merit review at a stated priority date (Emory, Drexel, GW, UIC, Albany). USF is the counterexample: the school itself tells internationals to arrive fully self-funded.
- **None of the 12 passes the board's hard filter** (full tuition + stipend guaranteed). Every route here is partial scholarship + work, exactly the Sakib shape: the gap must be coverable from assistantship wages within the F-1 rules above, and first-year funds must be documentable for the I-20/visa regardless of expected earnings.
- **Fee-waiver sequencing:** if the SOPHAS international waiver is used, the 14-day submit clock starts at approval — request it only when the file (WES ordered, referees briefed) is nearly ready.
