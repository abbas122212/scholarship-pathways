## What the session covers (from the legible parts)

- Sadab introduces Sakib: since March he has been a Clinical Research Coordinator in the Infectious Diseases division at Emory University School of Medicine, working on HIV-immunology research.
- Sakib describes feeling "completely lost" during internship, and starting his applications the November before his internship ended (his internship ran February to February).
- He pushes back on two common beliefs: that Bangladeshi doctors can only work unpaid/voluntarily in the USA, and that there are no scholarships for a Master's — he calls these partly myth and says the real gap is that students don't know the pathways (full-time job routes, visa application, funded Master's).
- Choose programmes that match your own area of interest and experience — his are maternal health, HIV and adolescent health — rather than chasing any scholarship in an unrelated field.
- IELTS: a minimum score is enough for selection, but a visa officer may look more favourably on an 8 than a 7; understand the actual reason if a visa is refused.
- Decide your destination early. He is blunt about the Middle East/Maldives route: little career growth, and he has seen seniors return after ~3 years to sit FCPS — the same effort is better spent on the US route.
- Research advice for students in Bangladesh: start with voluntary research; write case reports from atypical cases in third year, then cross-sectional studies, then cohort studies — prospective cohort work is the most valuable. Build a small community of like-minded people, attach a professor or senior to guide the first project, and give research at least an hour a day alongside medical life. His own first project was stuck for a year with no publication, but the publications came during internship.
- Closing: a call to build a stronger Bangladeshi doctor community abroad, with thanks and prayers for Sakib.
