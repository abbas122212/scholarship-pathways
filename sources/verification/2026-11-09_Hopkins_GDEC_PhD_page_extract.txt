## About the PhD in Global Disease Epidemiology and Control Program

The PhD program in Global Disease Epidemiology and Control, International Health is more applied, multidisciplinary, and internationally oriented than standard infectious disease epidemiology programs. It prepares students to take leadership positions in important global public health settings with strong research components.

This is a [STEM designated program](/offices-and-services/office-of-admissions-services/how-to-apply/stem-designated-programs "STEM Designated Programs"). Eligible F-1 visa students can receive an additional 24 months of work authorization, beyond the initial 12 months of post-completion Optional Practical Training (OPT).

## Admissions Requirements

For general admissions requirements, please visit the [How to Apply](/offices-and-services/office-of-admissions-services/how-to-apply "How to Apply") page. This specific program also requires:

### Prior Graduate Degree

A degree in medicine, veterinary medicine, or dentistry, or a master’s degree or equivalent graduate training in epidemiology, statistics, international health, tropical medicine, microbiology, parasitology, immunology, or virology.

### Prior Work Experience

Not required but highly desirable

### Standardized Test Scores

Standardized test scores **are required** for this program. This program accepts the following standardized test scores: GRE. **Applications will be reviewed holistically based on all application components.**

The Department will consider waiver requests only if the applicant submitted official GRE scores when they applied to the MSPH in International Health and were admitted to the program within 10 years of the date they are applying to the PhD. Waiver requests will need to be submitted by the applicant to [Cristina Salazar](mailto:csalazar@jhu.edu) by November 1 with the following information: a) full name, b) date of birth, c) year admitted to the MSPH degree program. A decision will be made and communicated to the applicant no later than November 7. Waivers will be reviewed and are not guaranteed.

## Tuition and Funding

PhD students at Johns Hopkins University are bargaining unit employees of the Teachers and Researchers United Union. Per the [Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA)](https://provost.jhu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/TRU-UE-Local-197-Johns-Hopkins-University-Contract-2024-2027-SIGNED.pdf) with the Union, PhD students are guaranteed a minimum annual stipend of $52,000 through June 2027. Stipend levels beyond that date will be subject to successor agreement negotiations between the University and the Union. Tuition, fees, and medical benefits are provided, including health insurance premiums for PhD student’s children and spouses of international students, depending on visa type. The minimum stipend and tuition coverage is guaranteed for at least the first four years of a BSPH PhD program; specific amounts and the number of years supported, as well as work expectations related to that stipend will vary across departments and funding source. Please refer to the [CBA](https://provost.jhu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/TRU-UE-Local-197-Johns-Hopkins-University-Contract-2024-2027-SIGNED.pdf) to review specific benefits, compensation, and other terms.
